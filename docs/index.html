A1

What this is, plainly.

Spindle is a personal, open-source project: put a record on, and the app identifies the exact pressing by listening to it, logs the play, scrobbles it to Last.fm, and later learns your taste to suggest what to spin and buy next.

I'm building it in the open to learn modern mobile, backend, and AI work end to end, and because the vinyl apps I found all make you do the boring part by hand. This page is the plan and the reasoning behind it, kept honest so anyone reading the code knows what they're looking at.

A2

The honest part: this space is busy.

Plenty of good apps already track vinyl. Pretending otherwise would be the fastest way to lose trust, so here's the field as I found it, and where Spindle actually differs.

Everyone else asks you to log the record. Spindle's bet is that the record can announce itself.

  • CrateAI collection manager; identifies records by photographing the cover.
  • Spun ItLog and share your spins; auto-scrobble on premium.
  • DiscrobbleFast Discogs-to-Last.fm scrobbling.
  • RecordzDiscovery and flipping through collections.
  • DeadwaxPressing research via runout / matrix codes.

Spindle's wager is the combination: continuous identification from the audio itself (not typing, not a cover photo), feeding an open, self-hostable log and an AI that works over your real history. If that combination turns out not to matter, this is still a project I learned a lot building.

B1

Two different jobs. The right tool for each.

Identifying which recording is playing and understanding why you like it are not the same problem, and today they need different tools.

Modern audio LLMs (OpenAI, Gemini) genuinely understand audio: transcription, lyrics, mood. But naming an exact recording is precise matching against a huge reference catalog, and general models are unreliable at it. In one published test, Gemini misidentified a clip of Dire Straits as a HAIM song1; notably, Gemini's own "what song is this?" hands the job to Google's fingerprinter rather than the model2. So Spindle rents the fingerprinter and saves the LLM for what it's good at.

Fingerprint

Which record is this?

AudD / ACRCloud match the audio against a reference catalog of well over 100M tracks3, down toward the specific release. Rented, because the catalog is the real barrier, not the algorithm.

Audio LLM

Why do you like it?

Claude and audio-capable models describe mood, texture, and feel, and reason over your listening history. This is where the recommendations and insights come from.

C1

Roadmap, smallest useful thing first.

  1. Web MVP: listen → identify NEXT
    A browser page that records a few seconds, calls the fingerprinter, and names the record. No database yet — a static page plus one proxy function on Vercel's free tier. Proves the core before any app store.
  2. Capture loop + scrobble
    Confirm the match, save the play, scrobble to Last.fm. The first genuinely useful version.
  3. Collection + profile
    Your logged plays become a collection and a public listening profile.
  4. Buy-next recommendations
    Claude over your history, filtered to in-print and in stock.
  5. Feed & social
    Follow people, see live spins. Added once solo use holds up.

D1

Commercial use.

Spindle ships free and open. If it ever grows into something more, the principle is fixed: the core stays free, and logging is never behind a paywall.

Logging, scrobbling, collection, and feed remain free. A Spindle+ tier would add the AI recommendations, insights, and continuous auto-listen, and "buy next" links may carry a marketplace referral. Affiliate margins on records are slim, so a subscription would likely carry any early revenue. This is set down now only so the intent is clear and on the record.
